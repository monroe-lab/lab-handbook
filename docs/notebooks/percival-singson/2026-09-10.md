## What I did

Master paper on grape feeder cultures: [Tricoli & Debernardi, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11184525/#sec6)

* Origin of feeder cells: <span style="color: #b55ad4">grape rootstock genotype 1103P (1103 Paulson)</span>
    * Originally bred in 1896, it is a cross between *Vitis berlandieri* cv. Rességuier number 2 and *Vitis rupestris* cv. Lot .
    * No published reference genome assembly for this rootstock cultivar.
    * Has high resistance to *Phylloxera,* Pierce's Disease and moderate resistance to cotton root rot. Has poor uptake of potassium from the soil. Tolerates high soil alkalinity and high salt content. Drought adapted. Moderate susceptibility to iron chlorosis (moderate iron uptake ability.) Doesn't do well in wet soil. Excellent root system regeneration.
    * Rootstock is clonal, but not immune to somaclonal variation. These cells were originally derived from previously generated embryogenic calli.
* Biweekly subcultures, where 10mL of the suspension is removed and replaced with an equal volume of medium, originally repeated 6 times to get the cells used to a high osmotic medium (period of required response plasticity.) Once the samples were acclimated, 4-5mL were transferred from a 7 day old suspension to an empty shake flask with 20mL of the medium added. 

* Yesterday's extraction was relatively successful. 
    * However the nano drop had a higher absorbance than it should around 230. This means that there may be residual phenols, salts, or ethanol. Likely ethanol left over from the washes because I did take out more volume than EB I put in. Need to be more careful of that. 
    * Qubit and Nanodrop results were relatively contridictory of each other. 
        * May want to do both high-sensitivity and broad range on the qubit with fresh blanks to get possibly more accurate results. 
        * Disparity is unlikely to be due to RNA since most of the cells are dead and there is an RNase treatment. May possibly be due to small DNA fragments not picked up by the qubit. 
        * Cannot yet tell what concentrations yield the best DNA because of the contradiction. Nanodrop suggests that the lower amounts of sample yield better (optimal amount for the reagents according to the nanodrop looks about 0.5mL, but according to the qubit it appears to be 1.5mL.)
